4. How a round works

  • Each round opens with a short betting window. You may place one bet per round during that window.
  • When the window closes the plane takes off and the multiplier rises from 1.00x.
  • Press Cash out before the round ends to win your bet multiplied by the multiplier at that moment.
  • If the round ends before you cash out, your bet is lost.
  • The server's clock decides when you cashed out and when the round ended. Delays on your device or network are at your own risk.

6. Fair play and the crash point

Before each round starts, the server publishes the SHA-256 hash of a secret seed. The seed decides the round's crash point. When the round ends the seed is revealed, so you can check on the game page that it matches the published hash and produces that crash point. No one chooses the seed's crash point.

7. The house limit (risk management)

To protect the game's operator, Aviator keeps a house balance. Every bet adds its stake to the house balance, and every cash-out takes its payout from it. The operator sets a minimum, called the floor, below which the house balance may not fall.

  • While the plane is flying, the server works out what it would cost to pay every bet still in the air at the current multiplier.
  • If paying those bets would take the house balance below the floor, the round ends at that multiplier. It can end before the seed's crash point.
  • Every bet still in the air when the round ends this way is lost, just as at any other end of a round. Bets already cashed out are paid in full.
  • A round ended by the house limit shows "HOUSE LIMIT REACHED". The round check shows both the crash point from the seed and the multiplier at which the round ended.
  • The house limit applies to all players in a round equally. It never looks at who placed a bet.

This means the most that can be won in a round depends on the house balance at that moment. When many players or large bets are in the air, a round may end at a lower multiplier than it otherwise would.
